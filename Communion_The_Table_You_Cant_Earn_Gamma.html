Communion · Cristo Viene

COMMUNION
The Table You Can't Earn

"The table isn't a reward for the clean. It's a meal for the forgiven."

Big Idea: You don't come to the table because you're good enough — you come because Jesus made you worthy.

"This is my body, which is for you; do this in remembrance of me... This cup is the new covenant in my blood; do this, whenever you drink it, in remembrance of me."

— 1 Corinthians 11:24–25

Hook

Do I Even Belong Here?

Be honest for a second:

  • Have you ever passed on communion because you felt like a hypocrite — like you'd messed up too much that week?
  • Have you ever taken the bread and cup while your mind was a thousand miles away, just going through the motions?
  • Have you ever felt like everyone else at church has it together and you're the fraud?

Today we deal with two opposite mistakes: feeling too unworthy to come to the table, and coming to it carelessly like it's nothing. There's a better way — and it runs straight through the cross.

The Psychology

Guilt Says "Oops." Shame Says "I'm not worthy."

Psychologists draw a sharp line between two feelings:

Guilt

"I did something bad." It points at the behavior — and it can actually move you to fix things.

Shame

"I am bad." It points at you — and it makes you want to hide, disappear, and pull away.

Here's the key: shame makes us hide. It's the exact instinct Adam and Eve had — mess up, then run and cover. So when we feel unworthy, our gut says pull back from God, skip the table, avoid the very place that could heal us.

Shame tells you to run from the table when the table is the one place designed to remind you that you're already accepted.

The Misconception

Not Good Enough? Exactly.

The Lie

"I can't take communion — I'm not good enough." Here's the twist: nobody is good enough. That's the whole point.

1 John 1:8

"If we claim to be without sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us."

Romans 8:1

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

The table was never for people who cleaned themselves up. It's for people who admit they couldn't — and trust that Jesus did it for them.

The reframe: You don't earn a seat at this table with your goodness. Jesus bought your seat with His blood. "Not good enough" isn't a reason to stay away — it's the reason communion exists.

Biblical Example

The Man Who Denied Him — At the Table

Situation

At the Last Supper, Jesus handed the bread and cup to His disciples — including Peter, who would swear loyalty that very night.

Struggle

Hours later, Peter denied even knowing Jesus — three times, to save his own skin — then wept bitterly. If anyone ever felt disqualified, it was Peter.

Decision

Peter could have run for good. Instead, after the resurrection, when Jesus came looking for him, Peter let himself be found — and Jesus restored him over breakfast: "Do you love me? ... Feed my sheep." (John 21)

The Lesson

Jesus served the first communion to the very people who were about to fail Him — and then came back for them. The table has always been for failures who let grace find them.

What Communion Is

What You're Actually Holding

Communion isn't a snack and it isn't a fancy ritual. It's a remembrance Jesus Himself gave us:

The Bread

His body, broken for you

The Cup

His blood, poured out to start a whole new covenant, a new relationship with God

 1 Corinthians 11:24-26

This is my body, which is for you; do this in remembrance of me.” 25 In the same way, after supper he took the cup, saying, “This cup is the new covenant in my blood; do this, whenever you drink it, in remembrance of me.” 26 For whenever you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes.

Why a physical, repeated ritual? Because your heart forgets. Every time you taste it, your body preaches the gospel back to you: I was that broken, I am that loved. And you don't do it alone — the whole church declares together, we all needed the same cross.

Modern Examples

Where This Trips Us Up

For us right now, it usually goes one of two ways:

Hiding (Shame)

  • Skipping communion because you sinned this week and feel like a fraud
  • Avoiding church after a big mess-up
  • Believing your secret makes you the one person grace doesn't cover

Going Through the Motions (Careless)

  • Taking the bread and cup while scrolling in your head
  • Treating it like a routine snack, zero thought
  • Showing up to church to be seen, not to meet God — like the Corinthians who made it all about themselves

Both miss it. One runs from the table; the other sleepwalks through it.

What God Says

What God Says

1 Corinthians 11:28

"Everyone ought to examine themselves before they eat of the bread and drink from the cup."

1 John 1:9

"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins."

Romans 3:23–24

"All have sinned and fall short... and are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that came by Christ Jesus."

Culture Says

"Clean yourself up first, then maybe you'll be worthy."

God Says

"Come honestly, examine your heart, and receive what I already made you — worthy."

The balance: Paul's warning about taking communion "in an unworthy manner" (1 Cor 11:27) is about how you come — carelessly, selfishly, with no thought for Christ — not about whether you are worthy enough. So examine your heart honestly, confess what's there... then come freely.

Identity Shift

From Fraud to Family

Stop asking: "Am I good enough to be at this table?" Start believing: "Jesus made me worthy — I belong at this table."

Self-examination isn't a shame spiral where you dig until you feel awful enough to earn forgiveness. It's honest confession — and then freedom. Once you've named it and handed it to God, the examination is over. You're not a guest hoping not to get caught; you're family, invited by name.

Insight: Shame says "clean yourself up before you come." Grace says "come, and I'll clean you up." Only one of those is the gospel.

Action Steps

How to Come to the Table

1

Examine — Don't Obsess

Ask God to show you what's really there. Be honest, not brutal.

2

Confess, Then Stop

Name your sin, receive forgiveness (1 John 1:9), and let the examination be over. Don't keep paying a debt Jesus already covered.

9 If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness.

3

Don't Skip Out of Shame

Feeling unworthy is the reason to come, not a reason to hide.

4

Don't Sleepwalk Through It

Slow down. Actually picture the cross. Let the bread and cup mean something.

5

Remember, Out Loud If You Can

Thank Jesus specifically for what His body and blood bought you.

Small Group Discussion
